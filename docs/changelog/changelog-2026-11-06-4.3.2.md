# 4.3.2 — Native HDR: real highlights from the game's own scene (experimental) (2026-11-06)

Built on 4.3.1.

## Why

HDR output expanded the finished 8-bit picture, so highlights were a guess. A diagnostic copy of the game's tone mapper logged its input at 4K: Central Yharnam reached 45 to 83 (66 to 122 after exposure) and Forbidden Woods 23 to 53, with about 0.1% of pixels above 1.0. That range was being thrown away.

## What changed

**Gain from the tone mapper** (`renderer_vulkan/native_hdr_gain.h`, `vk_native_hdr.cpp`, `vk_pipeline_cache.cpp`)
- The tone mappers with the curve `1 - exp(-K * c)` (pixel shaders 5e67d866, ccb08339 and three more) also write `E / (1 - exp(-E))`, with `E = K * largest channel`, to an R16F image at render size. It's 1 where the curve is linear and grows only where it compresses.
- K is read live, because it changes by area (0.98 in Central Yharnam, 2.24 in Forbidden Woods).
- The tone mapper's target stays 8-bit. FXAA, the 0..1 grading LUT, DLSS, the UI and frame generation all come after it and would clip or break on a float target.

**The HUD stays at paper white** (`native_hdr_stamp.comp`, `vk_temporal_upscaler.cpp`)
- The frame is copied before the UI draws. When the UI is finished, the gain goes into the frame's alpha, with no gain where the UI changed the picture.
- Over HUD pixels the gain reads a median of 1.00 and a maximum of 1.1. Relying on the UI's own alpha gave a median of 3.17 and a maximum of 16.9, because translucent UI lowers alpha.

**Presentation** (`post_process.frag`, `vk_presenter.cpp`)
- Linear SDR times the gain, a BT.2390 roll-off toward `hdr_peak_nits` on the largest channel in PQ, then ST.2084.
- The GPU test reads back 100.2 nits for white at a 100-nit paper white, 21.6 for mid-gray, and 999 for the largest gain at a 1000-nit peak.

**Frame generation** (`native_hdr_generated.comp`, `NativeHdrCarry` in `vk_native_hdr.cpp`)
- Frame generation interpolates the picture but not the alpha holding the gain. Each generated pixel takes the gain of whichever game frame it looks like, so a still pixel matches the game frames exactly and the UI stays at no gain.
- A pixel too dark for its gain, as happens around fast movement, gets only what its SDR level allows. Without this, fast attacks bled white.

**Heavy attacks** (`post_process.frag`)
- The scene's range is far wider than the display's room above paper white, so the gain is log-compressed into that room. Without this, a heavy attack's flash pinned flat white at the peak.
- Gain below 4x stays the SDR picture, which keeps a flash's soft cloud from glowing, and the boost has a limit (2.5x by default), so a lower paper white doesn't bleed more.
- The overlay's Native HDR curve shows the curve and tunes paper white, peak, the boost limit, the curve and highlight color while playing.
- HDR and Native HDR switch from the same menu while playing. Changes reach `bbport.ini` when a control is let go, and `last_run.log` gets an `HDR settings:` line for each.

**Launcher** (`bbport_launcher_backend.py`, `bbport_launcher_win.py`)
- Visuals, HDR: "Native HDR (experimental)", off by default, saved as `hdr_native`.
- Paper white and peak tuned in the overlay are kept. They used to snap to the nearest row value when the launcher read `bbport.ini`, and the next write saved that; now they're clamped as the game clamps them (80 to 800, 400 to 4000). The row shows such a value as "137 nits (custom)", and highlight color likewise. Highlight boost still snaps.
- At launcher start `bbport.ini`'s `hdr_white_nits`, `hdr_peak_nits`, `hdr_native` and `hdr_native_color` win over the saved JSON, which is older when the overlay changed them. "My settings" still overrides them when saved.

**DLSS 5 add-on setting** (`bbport_launcher_win.py` `apply_renodx_nr`, `bbport_launcher_backend.py`)
- `NRPaperWhiteScale` in `bin\ReShade.ini` is recorded in the launcher's settings on each Play, and written back when the file lacks it, as after a fresh kit copy (`dlss5-setup.ps1`). It has no launcher row; the add-on's panel still sets it.

**HDR metadata** (`renderer_vulkan/hdr_metadata.h`, `vk_swapchain.cpp`, `vk_instance.cpp`, `vk_presenter.cpp`)
- With Native HDR on and `VK_EXT_hdr_metadata` available, an HDR swapchain gets BT.2020 primaries, D65 white, and the resolved peak as max luminance and MaxCLL. Min luminance is 0 and MaxFALL 0 (unknown).
- Sent once for each new swapchain and again when the peak changes, at most every 0.25 s while the overlay's slider moves, because the call's cost is up to the driver. `last_run.log` gets one `HDR metadata:` line, saying it was sent or that the extension is absent.

**Saved HDR settings** (`gpu/shim/bbport_settings.cpp`)
- Once `hdr_native`, `hdr_native_color`, `hdr_white_nits` or `hdr_peak_nits` is in `bbport.ini`, the game keeps writing it, also at its default. A value dropped on going back to the default came back from the launcher's saved copy at its next start, so Native HDR switched off in game turned on again.

## Limits

- A highlight moving fast between two game frames can get less gain on the generated frame.
- Highlight color (`hdr_native_color`, launcher: Native HDR highlight color) rebuilds color from the SDR picture, so it's an estimate. Low is the default.
- It needs upscaling above 1080p. The native 1080p path gets no gain.
- Tone mapper variants with another curve or none get no gain. In-game runs exercised 5e67d866 and 5c02eaf8.
- The gain is jittered at render size and DLSS's output isn't, so bright edges may shimmer. This hasn't been judged on a physical display.
- With the setting off, the GPU test still reads 742.757 nits for neutral white, as before. In-game frames weren't compared byte for byte.
- HDR metadata's effect on a display is unverified: scripted runs log it as sent, but it hasn't been judged on a physical display, and a display or the driver may ignore it.
- `NRPaperWhiteScale` is recorded only on Play, so a change made in the add-on's panel in the last session of a folder that's then rebuilt isn't carried. With two `-dlss5` folders, the last one played sets the recorded value.

## DLSS 5 stability

Neither of these is tied to Native HDR: both happened with it on and off. All runs are on an RTX 4090 with DLSS 5 and frame generation.

- **Resizing the window (fixed):** making the swapchain again could free the DLSS 5 bridge's imported images while the bridge's copies were being recorded or not yet submitted, which showed up as a device lost or a fault in `nvoglv64.dll`. The DLSS hook and the submission of its work now hold a lock that making the swapchain again also takes (`dlss_bridge_gate`, `vk_dlss.h`). Scripted window shrinks at 4K output crashed 4 of 6 runs on this build before the fix and 3 of 6 on 4.3.1; with the fix, 0 of 9 (6 Native HDR on, 3 off), plus 0 of 2 with 8 more grow and shrink cycles. The bridge's own side comes from its logs and crash stacks, not its source.
- **Lamp travel at 4K (not fixed):** before the resize fix, 3 of 5 scripted runs failed (two froze while loading a lamp, one faulted in the game's heap free). These runs weren't repeated with the fix, and no earlier version was run for comparison. With DLSS 4.5, 1 of 10 faulted, with Native HDR on and no frame generation, and it didn't repeat.

## Cost

Native HDR on against off at 4K, the same stops on the same route:

| Condition | Game FPS | Frame time |
|---|---|---|
| DLSS 4.5, frame generation | 94.1 against 98.6 | +0.48 ms, slower at 8 of 8 stops |
| DLSS 5, frame generation | 36.4 against 36.8 | +0.30 ms, within the noise between runs |
| DLSS 4.5, no frame generation | 114.2 against 114.4 | Hidden by the 120 FPS limit at 6 of 8 stops |
