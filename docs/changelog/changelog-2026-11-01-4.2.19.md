# 4.2.19 — Thunderwright's Kirkhammer gets a rune-iron head (2026-11-01)

Built on 4.2.18 (made apart from its Thunderwright balance, storm look and damage log, then put on top of them).

## Why

Thunderwright's Kirkhammer looked exactly like the Kirkhammer. It now gets a hammer head of its own in the spirit of Norse myth's Mjölnir (not any modern film or comic design), keeping the Kirkhammer's shape: the stone block as dark iron with Elder Futhark runes and Norse knotwork. The release ships no game files, so the textures are made at start from the player's own Kirkhammer textures, and that needed a reader and writer for the PS4 texture format, which the repo did not have.

## What changed

**PS4 texture converter** (`bloodborne_pc/scripts/ps4_texture.py`, new)
- Reads a PS4 TPF's BCn textures (BC1, BC3, BC4, BC5) to Pillow images and writes them back with the same headers, sizes and layout. Each mip level is 1D-tiled: 8x8-block micro tiles row by row, blocks in Morton order inside a tile (the GCN thin micro tiling in `gpu/shadps4/video_core/host_shaders/tiling.comp`), every level padded to whole tiles. The texture header's last word is the DXGI format.
- Byte-exact round trip on the Kirkhammer's own textures, both detail levels: detile then tile gives back the same bytes, and so does decoding an image and setting it back unchanged. `set_image()` keeps every block whose pixels did not change and makes a mip level again only where the full-size image changed, so an edit costs only the blocks it touches. New blocks are Pillow's BC1/BC3/BC5 encoder's; BC4 comes from the alpha half of a BC3 block (the same 8-byte format).
- `rename()` renames textures in place (same length).

**Rune-iron textures** (`bloodborne_pc/scripts/thunderwright/rune_iron.py`, new)
- The Kirkhammer's parts share one texture set between the sword and the hammer. Only the part of the UV square below the blade (`HEAD_TOP`) changes, so the sword is untouched.
- Albedo: the stone made dark iron from its own brightness (the grime pattern kept), its rust and old blood kept over it; the inscription on the two broad faces smoothed away. Inlay: dull, worn silver (toned down after the first preview, to read as engraving).
- Normal map: the inlay carved in as grooves, made from Sobel slopes of the inlay. The map's convention was read off the block's convex faces (red grows as the surface tilts to the texture's left, green as it tilts up). Pillow's 3x3 kernel takes its rows bottom-up, so the y kernel is written upside down.
- Specular and gloss: the old pattern pulled into a metal's range, with the inlay bright.
- Design: Elder Futhark (public domain) as stroke data, all 24 runes. ᚦᚢᚾᚱᚨᛉ large down each broad face, the futhark in two small columns, a braid frame with knot corners; a braid band on the narrow side face. Drawn once at 2048x1024 and fitted to each texture, so the low-detail set matches.
- `python scripts/thunderwright/rune_iron.py <game> <out>` writes before/after previews (a rough lit render, plus every texture).

**Thunderwright's own model** (`scripts/thunderwright/weapon.py`)
- Model 1032 (unused): `parts/wp_a_1032.partsbnd.dcx` and `_l`, copied from the Kirkhammer's 1031 (every row of the Kirkhammer, Kirkhammer +x and its Uncanny/Lost forms uses 1031). Same meshes, part animations (the transform) and collision. Only the texture names change, `WP_A_1031_*` to `WP_A_1032_*`, in the texture archive and in each mesh's four texture paths (same length, so no offsets move), so the two weapons never share a loaded texture. Bone and mesh names stay, since the part animations bind to them.
- `add_params()` sets `equipModelId` 1032 on the eleven rows; `verify()` allows it and checks it, and that no game row used 1032.
- `verify_model()` checks the copy against the Kirkhammer's parts: the same parts and names (renamed), meshes changed only in their texture paths, textures of the same formats and sizes, the sword's share of every texture byte-identical and the rest changed.

**Glowing runes** (`bloodborne_pc/scripts/flver.py`, new; `ps4_texture.py`, `rune_iron.py`, `weapon.py`)
- The port's ray tracing does ambient occlusion and moon shadows only, so the glow comes from the game's own materials instead: the Kirkhammer's `P[Weapon]_[ARSN]_NoCM` draws `GXFlver_ColDifSpcBumpIbl_Bloody`, and `C[ARSN]_em_Glow` draws the same shader plus an emissive texture and bloom (`GXFlver_ColDifSpcBumpEmiIblGlow_Bloody`; c1040 and c1210 use it, armour 9750 a player-part variant). The meshes move onto it: the MTD path, an emissive texture entry after the detail normal map (`g_EmissiveTexture`, as the game's glow materials list it) and the GX list's two glow items (`GX03` glow scale 1.0 after `GX00`, `GX32` zeros before `GX30`), the same as c1040's material.
- `flver.py` reads FLVER2 and makes that edit: one texture entry inserted, every absolute offset past it moved, the new strings and GX list after the old header data, the vertex and index data moved unchanged. `check_glow()` checks that nothing else changed.
- `Ps4Tpf.add_texture()` adds a texture to a TPF (laid out again: entries, names, data from a 0x100 boundary). The emissive texture (`WP_A_1032_e`, `_e_l`) is BC1 sRGB, the albedo's size: black, with the rune design in blue-white.

**Hunter's Arsenal** (`enhancements.py`, `mods.py`)
- The layer now also holds `parts/wp_a_1032` and `_l`. `mods.py` reports a mod above it that replaces them. `ps4_texture.py` is part of the layer's identity (and the merge layer's), so a change to it remakes the weapons.

## How it was tested

- `tests/test_ps4_texture.py` (new): tiling both ways on random data; Morton order; the 2048x1024 BC1 chain size the game uses; with `BB_TEST_GAME_DIR`, the byte-exact round trip of both Kirkhammer texture sets, a small edit changing only its four blocks, and a rename keeping the layout.
- `tests/test_flver.py` (new): every Kirkhammer mesh, both detail levels, moved onto the glow material and checked, refused a second time, and its material the same shape as c1040's (texture slots and GX list). `tests/test_ps4_texture.py`: a texture added beside the others, which stay byte-identical.
- `tests/test_thunderwright.py`: the whole futhark and the design staying on the hammer head; with the dump, the made files including the rune-iron model (checked by `verify_model()`), Thunderwright's rows on 1032 and every Kirkhammer row still on 1031, the plain copy texel-identical to the Kirkhammer, and Hunter's Arsenal's layer holding both weapons' models.
- Previews reviewed before the build.
- Played on 4.2.19 (DLSS 5, 4K HDR, Hunter's Dream): the head shows the runes the right way round (ᚦᚢᚾᚱᚨᛉ reads top to bottom) with the braid frame and side band. In daylight the iron read as bright steel, so its albedo was darkened by about a third and its specular and gloss lowered; the runes keep theirs.
- In game: see the release's test notes (the rune-iron head on Thunderwright's Kirkhammer only; the plain Kirkhammer still stone).
